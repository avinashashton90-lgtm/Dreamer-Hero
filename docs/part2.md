# Part 2 — Gearhaven (reference; add new pass notes at the end of the Fire list)

### Part 2 (Gearhaven — see STORY.md; built so far)
1. **Arrival** — AJ and the horse drop out of the light into a brass courtyard under a sky of slow-turning gears (the horse now wears a brass wind-up key — its brass armour plates are a gear reward); Tick, a small floating brass robot with two sky-blue eyes, welcomes him: AJ "Where am I?" — Tick "Welcome to Gearhaven." — AJ, looking up at the Dream Clock: "Am I inside a clock?" — Tick: "It's the Dream Clock. And it's slowing down." / "If it stops, your dream ends. Five elements can restart it." / "But someone took them. It's her doing." — AJ "Amy?" — Tick, nervously: "I can't say." (Tick never uses witch wording in Part 2 dialogue.)
2. **The courtyard** — five element doors; objective "Find the Fire Element".
3. **The Gearhaven ride** (behind the Fire door; the first playable section) — on the horse through a brass city: grind rails, steam vents launching the horse high (the first up onto a conveyor belt hanging over the street), swinging hammers, turning cog platforms and conveyor belts, brass gears to collect (a third, two thirds and all of them unlock gear rewards), checkpoints, to the Fire Element's door.
4. **Element levels** (Fire, Lightning, Steam, Magnet, Time), **Amy's final fight** — stubs ("coming soon") for now (the Fire Element's door at the ride's end opens its stub).
5. **"To be continued... Part 3"** — the Part 2 ending screen.


- Level manager (`levelManager.js` `LevelManager`): the game's chapters — `part1` (main.js's own loop and `World`; its `enter()` takes the hero and horse back into the world's scene and restores the riders) and `part2` (built by `src/part2/index.js` `createPart2(shared)`, **imported on demand** into its own chunk, so nothing in Part 2 can break Part 1's start). A chapter is `{ id, scene, enter(opts), exit(), update?(dt, realDt, ctx) }`; `levels.load(id, { start })` builds it on first use (a chapter that fails to load returns null and the game stays where it is), runs the old one's `exit()` and the new one's `enter()`, one switch at a time. main.js's loop runs Part 1 as before while `levels.id === 'part1'`; otherwise the chapter's `update()` (it drives its own input, hero, camera and cutscenes) and renders its scene — the state machine, HUD, input, camera, cutscene player (`cinematic`, the reusable `playCutscene`), sound (soundscape: `musicPhase` picks a chapter's music) and `DIALOGUE` are shared. A new game (the intro), Play Again and the test menu's jumps load `part1` first; the ending screen shows Part 2's titles while in Part 2.
- Model kits (`kits.js`, `CONFIG.kits`; sources and licences in CREDITS.md): Kenney's **Factory Kit** (`public/models/gearhaven/factory/glb/`), **Coaster Kit** (`…/rails/glb/`) and **Castle Kit** (`public/models/castle/glb/`) — CC0, small glb files sharing one palette texture per kit (`Textures/colormap.png`) — and the gear props (`public/models/gearhaven/props/`: `gear_01` (Poly by Google, CC-BY), `gear_cluster`, `gear_spiky` (via Poly Pizza; no attribution file came with them), `gear_collectible` (Quaternius, CC0)). Only the pieces a level uses are loaded, when it is built (Part 2's in its own chunk; the castle's the first time the desert is drawn), each cached; textures referenced by a piece are never fetched (the kit's one texture is loaded once and shared). The palettes are re-tinted in one Lambert shader (`kitMaterial`, `kits.tints`): the Factory Kit's orange → brass, blue → copper, its slate greys → bronze (`factory`); the Coaster Kit (it has only the one colormap — no gold or copper variant): its Monorail track brass (`railBrass`), its Steel track copper (`railCopper`), the supports dark bronze (`railSupports`); the Castle Kit brass for Gearhaven's skyline (`skyline`), its own colours for Part 1's castle; the Factory Kit's `variation-a` is another cool palette, so the colormap is used. Static pieces are merged into a few meshes per material and stretch (`mergeStatic`), turning gears instanced, multi-part placeholders one vertex-coloured mesh (`colorMerge`). Any piece that fails → that part's code-built placeholder (the tests check both).
